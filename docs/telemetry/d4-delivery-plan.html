Harness telemetry · decision D4

Getting the collector built

Attribution now lives in a file on a laptop. Until those files are gathered somewhere, every harness number describes exactly one machine. This is the plan to close that — three decisions, one pilot, four build workstreams, about a quarter.

Status Awaiting decisions — no build started Blocks Nine of the ten metrics at programme scale Duration ~13 weeks from decision date Owner [name]

01 Why this is the blocking item

The instrumentation is written and shipped. Hooks record which commits the harness helped produce, harness_metrics.py reads them back, and the /gate command captures accept/edit/reject per stage. None of it leaves the machine it runs on.

That was a deliberate choice — revision 2 moved AI attribution out of public git history, because most CPP repositories are public and a marker in a commit message is permanent. The cost of that choice is this plan. A report that says 38% assisted while covering 40 of 120 developers is not a small error; it is a different claim.

Six of the ten metrics come from Azure DevOps, Jira and SonarQube and need no collector at all. What the collector buys is the rest: adoption, per-skill usage, gate rejection rate, and — through the branch_pushed join — the ability to split cycle time, review turnaround, change failure and CI first-pass into assisted against unassisted. Without that split the harness metrics describe the programme, not the harness.

02 The gate — three decisions

None of these are engineering questions, and each one changes the code. Nothing should be written until all three are answered.

QuestionOwnerWhat it changesAnswered when
How long is telemetry retained? Measurement programme + IG Workspace retention configuration, and whether an archive tier is needed for year-on-year comparison. A number, in months, written down.
Who may query it, and who may purge it? Measurement programme + platform The RBAC model, and whether the deletion promise made to developers can actually be honoured centrally. Two named groups, not two named people.
Can a dedicated Log Analytics workspace be created, and in which subscription? Platform / landing zone owners Everything. See below. A subscription and a resource group.

The third question can stop the plan

If the answer is “share an existing workspace”, do not proceed to build — re-open the design first. A shared workspace means anyone holding reader on it can read developer telemetry, and it means deletion cannot be scoped without touching unrelated data. A workspace of its own is what turns “we hold this for twelve months, these named groups can query it, and here is how it is deleted” from an assurance into a configuration.

Two governance items on the same path

Neither is strictly D4, but both must land before rollout and both have lead times measured in weeks rather than days.

  • The disclosure question (D3) — what teams are told, and when, about what is recorded. The team-facing note is drafted and published; it needs sign-off from whoever owns the measurement programme, because it commits to things teams will hold the programme to.
  • Consultation — departmental trade union side and staff engagement, before programme-wide rollout. Five minutes now is far cheaper than retrofitting consent onto six months of data.

03 The schedule

Three streams run before the gate and are worth starting now; four run after it. Weeks are relative to the decision date, not to today.

04 Before the gate

0a

Baseline extract start now

Three to six months of pre-harness cycle time, change failure rate and defect escape, pulled from Azure DevOps and Jira. This does not depend on D4, on the pilot, or on anything else in this plan.

It should start regardless of how D4 lands. Every comparison the measurement framework makes is meaningless without it, and the history is cleanest now while adoption is still narrow — once the harness is widespread there is no clean “before” left to extract.

Effort 3–5 days Owner whoever holds ADO + Jira query access Depends on nothing
Exit A stored dataset, with its extraction query, that the monthly job can later compare against.
0b

Decisions and consultation blocking

The three questions in section 02, plus the D3 disclosure sign-off and the union-side conversation. Run them concurrently rather than in series — the workspace question in particular has a platform-team queue in front of it.

Effort 2–3 weeks elapsed Owner measurement programme owner Depends on nothing
Exit Retention in months, two named groups, a subscription and resource group, and a recorded consultation outcome.
0c

Azure workspace request lead time

Raise the request as soon as 0b answers the third question. Dedicated Log Analytics workspace, private link scope, retention set, RBAC groups attached. Terraform, and the programme already has the patterns — 64 azurerm_log_analytics_workspace declarations and azurerm_monitor_data_collection_rule already in use.

Not Splunk, which is the security and audit sink; routing developer-behaviour telemetry there sends a signal the programme does not mean, and would very likely make deletion impossible by design. Not Application Insights, which is built for application performance and gives weaker retention and purge control over what is, in substance, a research dataset.

Effort ~2 days of work, 4 weeks elapsed Owner platform team Depends on 0b question three
Exit An empty workspace exists, with retention and RBAC applied.
0d

Manual pilot must precede the build

Five to ten volunteers, each running harness_metrics.py --format json locally and sending the output by hand. It does not scale and is not meant to.

What it buys is finding out whether these are the right ten metrics, and how badly real rebase, squash-merge and cherry-pick behaviour degrades attribution in practice — before a quarter is spent on a pipeline that measures the wrong thing accurately. Include at least one cpp-ui-* repo, to prove husky chaining across post-commit and pre-push, and confirm the merge strategy per repo.

Effort 4 weeks elapsed, ~2 days each Owner harness owner Depends on volunteers, not on 0b
Exit A written pilot report: which metrics proved useful, the observed index-health numbers, and any metric to drop or redefine before it is built into a pipeline.

05 After the gate — four workstreams

A, B and C are one shipping path and should be built by the same pair. D is independent of all three and can run alongside from day one.

A

The shipper

A SessionEnd hook plus a daily catch-up, reading unshipped lines against a watermark, queueing locally when offline and retrying. The SessionEnd wiring already exists in settings.example.json; this is a second script alongside harness-telemetry.sh.

Hard constraint: it is never called from the git hooks. A network call on the commit path puts a VPN drop or a slow endpoint between a developer and their own commit. That property has been protected through every revision of this design and is not negotiable for convenience.

The local JSONL stays the source of truth. That is also what keeps rm -rf ~/.cpp-harness an honest deletion rather than a partial one.

Effort ~1 week Weeks 7–8 Depends on B's endpoint contract
Exit A laptop offline for a week ships everything on reconnect, with no duplicates and no gaps, and a killed endpoint never affects a commit.
B

The ingest service easiest to get wrong

A small service on the mgmt gateway. It authenticates the developer through Entra, checks group membership, discards the identity, and forwards to the Logs Ingestion API under its own workload identity. Deployed the same way the environment dashboard is, and profiled well below it — that runs at 100m CPU and 128Mi under two hundred concurrent users, and this does strictly less work.

Posting straight to a data collection endpoint needs no service at all and will be proposed as a simplification. Reject it. A laptop cannot use Managed Identity, and the hard rule against SAS tokens, connection strings and account keys rules out every shared-secret route — so the developer authenticates as themselves either way. Direct posting puts that identity at the front door, and “we do not know who sent this” becomes a statement about which table we query rather than about what exists.

The difference between not stored and not collected is exactly the difference this revision was written to protect. Rebuilding identifiability at the ingest layer by accident would be a worse outcome than never having moved attribution out of the commit at all.

Effort 2–3 weeks Weeks 7–9 Depends on 0c workspace
Exit A load test confirms the profile, and a review confirms no caller identity is written to the workspace, logged by the service, or recoverable from its traces.
C

Azure build-out

Data collection rule and endpoint, table schema, private link, workload identity for the ingest service, purge runbook. Follows the workspace from 0c. Mostly a platform-team ticket against existing patterns rather than new work.

The purge runbook is the part that is easy to defer and should not be: the deletion promise made to developers is only credible if someone has actually executed it once, on test data, and written down how long it took.

Effort 2–3 weeks Weeks 7–9 Depends on 0c
Exit A test record ships end to end, and a rehearsed purge removes it.
D

The monthly job largest piece

A scheduled Azure DevOps pipeline on MDV-ADO-AGENTS-01, which already holds ADO, Jira and Sonar access. It pulls the six metrics harness_metrics.py deliberately refuses to compute, joins branch_pushed to PR source branches for the assisted/unassisted split, and writes to Grafana at grafana.mgmt01.dev.nl.cjscp.org.uk. No new presentation tool.

Write it knowing telemetry arrives in bursts. The mgmt gateway is internal, so an empty day is not evidence of an idle day — the job must not read a quiet Friday as a drop in adoption.

Every report it produces states its own coverage. Speed metrics render next to their counter-metrics or not at all: cycle time beside change failure rate, review turnaround beside comments per PR.

Effort 3–4 weeks Weeks 7–11 Depends on 0a baseline, not on A–C
Exit One month of real data renders on the dashboard, with coverage stated, and reconciles against the pilot's hand-collected numbers.

07 Done means

  • A developer commits, closes their session, and the event reaches the workspace without ever having waited on a network call.
  • A monthly report covers more than one machine and states what share of eligible developers it covers, on the report itself, every month.
  • Cycle time, review turnaround, change failure rate and CI first-pass render split assisted against unassisted, each next to its counter-metric.
  • Someone has executed a purge on test data and written down what it removed and how long it took.
  • A reviewer with workspace access cannot identify a developer from the data, and a reviewer of the ingest service can confirm the caller identity is discarded rather than stored.
  • CPP_HARNESS_TELEMETRY=off still suppresses everything, and the opt-out rate is reported rather than engineered around.

08 What this still will not fix

Worth stating now, because it shapes how the first reports are read.

  • Coverage stays a caveat, not a solved problem. A report covers the machines that reported. Untracked must never be read as unassisted, and the reverse reading will be made by someone in the first month unless the report is designed to prevent it.
  • Bursty arrival. The mgmt gateway is internal, so telemetry lands when people connect. Daily granularity is not trustworthy; weekly is.
  • Squash-merge and cherry-pick still lose attribution. The PR-level join recovers squash-merge; cherry-pick has no fix, because git does not fire post-rewrite for it. It is an accepted undercount, and it biases against the harness, which is the safer direction.
  • Uneven install looks identical to non-use. The reader prints the core.hooksPath check for exactly this reason, and the collector does not change it.

09 The ask

Two things, and the second is free.

Answer the three questions in section 02, or say who should. Until they are answered the build cannot start, and the instrumentation already installed on developers' machines accumulates data nobody can read.

Let the baseline extract start now. It is three to five days of work, it depends on nothing in this plan, and it gets harder every week adoption widens. If D4 is ultimately declined, the baseline is still worth having.
